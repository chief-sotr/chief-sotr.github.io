## Topic 1: Sedna — The Myth and Its Meaning

### Overview
Sedna is a figure from Inuit mythology whose story Pam uses as the archetypal key to this entire transit. The myth encodes betrayal by the patriarchy, the experience of victimhood and scarcity, and — crucially — a complete metamorphosis into sovereignty and abundance. Pam reads the myth as a template for what humanity is being invited to do right now.

### Pam's Exact Words
"She had to let go of what she thought was tangible security of the past. The boat had always been tangible security. And we may have to do that too — we may have to release some of the old ways, the old systems, that so-called tangible security, in order to discover a whole new state of being."

"This myth of Sedna isn't just that she transformed in any kind of linear gradual way. It was a complete change of state. It was a complete metamorphosis — which is what we are going through now as humanity as we move further into the light body and become more crystalline."

"This is really a myth for our time."

### Key Framework/Analogy
**The Transmutation Arc:** Sedna's story moves through three stages that mirror humanity's current journey:
1. **Victimhood & Scarcity** — Betrayed by her husband (who failed his promises) and her father (who sacrificed her to save himself). This maps to the betrayal of the patriarchal systems humanity has depended on.
2. **The Fall into the Deep** — Forced surrender of tangible security (the boat, the familiar). The apparent catastrophe.
3. **Rebirth as Sea Goddess** — Complete metamorphosis; her fingers become dolphins, whales, and new sea creatures. Scarcity transmutes into abundance. Victim becomes sovereign creator.

### Examples
- Her husband "promised many things within the marriage and he failed to deliver on all of his promises" — scarcity and broken promises of the old system.
- Her father threw her from the kayak to save himself — betrayal of the patriarchy choosing self-preservation over care.
- Her fingers becoming dolphins and whales — destruction of one form becomes the seed of a richer creation.
